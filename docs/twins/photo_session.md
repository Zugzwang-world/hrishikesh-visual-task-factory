# Physical twins: photo session

12 layouts, three photos each, about 60 minutes. You need the printed mat, the eight shopping-list items, the layout sheets (layouts.pdf), a phone, a tape measure and a box or a stack of books. A printable copy of this guide is [photo_session.pdf](photo_session.pdf).

## Once, before you start

1. Print docs/twins/mat_A3.pdf at 100% (actual size, no 'fit to page'). Check that both 10 cm bars on the mat measure exactly 10 cm with a ruler.
2. Tape the mat flat on a plain table, front edge toward you (the edge that says FRONT EDGE). Tape along the paper's edges so it does not curl, never over the black corner squares.
3. Light: daylight or ordinary room light. No desk lamp pointing at the mat, no flash. Do not let your own shadow fall on the mat.
4. Phone camera: the normal 1x lens, no zoom, no portrait mode, no filters, held sideways (landscape). If it is an iPhone, set Settings, Camera, Formats to Most Compatible so photos are JPEG (HEIC also works on this Mac).
5. Lay out the eight items from the shopping list and the layout sheets (docs/twins/layouts.pdf): one page per layout, page 2 is L01.

## The three phone spots

The same three spots for every layout. Measure heights from the table top to the camera lens.

1. **Front photo** (in front of the mat, looking down at it).
   - Phone straight in front of the mat, in line with its centre, about 28 cm in front of the mat's front edge.
   - Camera lens about 50 cm above the table (a box or a stack of books works).
   - Tilt the phone down about 50 degrees, so the middle of the mat is in the middle of the screen.
2. **Overhead photo** (straight above the mat centre).
   - Phone flat, camera pointing straight down, right above the middle of the mat.
   - Camera lens about 60 cm above the table. Hold it, or rest it on something that spans the table.
   - Top of the phone toward the mat's back edge (the edge with the title).
3. **Side photo** (at the front-right corner, looking across).
   - Phone at the mat's front-right corner: about 30 cm to the right of the centre line and 16 cm in front of the front edge.
   - Camera lens about 42 cm above the table.
   - Point it at the middle of the mat, tilted down about 45 degrees.

## Rules for every photo

1. All four black corner squares must be fully in every photo, uncovered and sharp.
2. Close is good enough: the phone does not have to be exactly on its spot. The computer works out where the phone was from the corner squares, and renders the digital twin from there.
3. Objects must be where the sheet says, within about a centimetre: the cross on the sheet is the middle of the object, the red arrow is the way its front faces. Round fruit can face any way.
4. Nothing else on the mat: no phone, hands, cables or labels.

## For each layout

1. Clear the mat.
2. Open the layout's sheet page. Put the bowl down first if the layout has one, then every other object with its middle on its spot. Anything marked 'in the bowl' just goes into the bowl.
3. Take three photos: front, overhead, side (the spots above). One photo each is enough; retake one if it is blurred.
4. Tick the layout off and go to the next one.

## After the last layout

1. Copy all the photos to the Mac into the folder twins/inbox/ inside the project folder (AirDrop to Downloads, then move them). Names do not matter: the computer recognises which layout and which spot each photo shows. If you like, name them L01_front.jpg, L01_overhead.jpg, L01_side.jpg and so on.
2. Tell Claude the photos are in. It checks every photo (gate G8: markers found, phone position worked out, every coloured object within 2 cm of its spot; white ones are checked by eye), renders each photo's digital twin from the same spot, and lists any photo to retake.
